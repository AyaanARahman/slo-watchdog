## Scenarios

### fast-burn — PASS

A severe outage. 40% of requests failing is far above the 14.4x fast-burn page threshold of 7.2%, so the availability page must fire quickly. Latency is left untouched, so both latency alerts must stay silent - errors are injected with latency_ms=0, which is exactly why the two SLOs can be violated independently.

- **Injected:** `{'error_rate': 0.4}`
- **Load:** 40 rps for 150s

| `BudgetApiLatency/ticket` | must stay silent | stayed silent |

### slow-burn — PASS

A slow leak, and the most important scenario in the suite. 1.2% errors sits above the 1x ticket threshold (0.5%) but below both the 3x ticket threshold (1.5%) and the 14.4x page threshold (7.2%). So it must open a ticket and must NOT page.
A naive single-threshold rule pages here, and somebody loses sleep over something that could have waited until Tuesday. The negative assertion is the whole point.
1.2% is chosen deliberately rather than 1.5%: 1.5% sits exactly on the 3x boundary, and a test parked on a threshold boundary flaps.

- **Injected:** `{'error_rate': 0.012}`
- **Load:** 60 rps for 180s

### spike-recover — PASS

A total but brief outage: 100% errors for 60 seconds, then healthy again. The page must fire during the spike, and the signal must then recover once clean traffic flushes the short window.
Recovery is asserted on the SLI recording rule rather than on the alert state, and that distinction is the finding this scenario exists to capture. The page alert is an OR of two burn-rate branches; the 14.4x branch (5m AND 1h) clears in about five minutes, but the 6x branch (30m AND 6h) holds the alert up for roughly thirty. So "did it recover promptly?" has to name a branch. Asserting bare alert state would either wait 30 minutes or fail for a reason that is correct behaviour.

- **Injected:** `{'error_rate': 1.0}`
- **Load:** 40 rps for 60s

| `BudgetApiLatency/page` | must stay silent | stayed silent |

## How to read the detection times

These numbers are measured on a Prometheus whose windows are **not full**.
`rate()` computes over the samples actually present, not over the window's
nominal length, so on a young server a `rate(...[1h])` may cover only a few
minutes of data and every window collapses toward the same value.

In steady state, with a full hour of clean traffic already in the window, a
40% error rate needs `0.072 x 60 / 0.40 = 10.8 minutes` to drag the 1h
average above the 14.4x page threshold. The 5m window crosses in 54 seconds,
but the `AND` waits for the slower of the two.

**So treat these as lower bounds.** They prove the rules fire on the right
signal and stay quiet on the wrong one; they are not steady-state SLAs for
time-to-detect.

## Scenario isolation

Each scenario deletes this service's time series before it starts, via the
Prometheus admin API. Without it, scenario 1's 40% error rate remains inside
the 30m/1h/6h windows and scenario 2's *must not page* assertion fails --
not because the alerting is wrong, but because the windows work exactly as
designed. Same discipline as truncating a database between integration tests.
